## The idea

It started the way most of my bad ideas do: with a confident sentence. *How hard can a bench be?*

{% include figure.html src="bench-plan.jpg" alt="Scanned notebook page with boxy sketches of a bench." caption="The plan. Notice the part where I wrote 'figure out joints later'." kind="scan" align="right" %}

I drew it on lined paper, labelled the dimensions, and then spent forty minutes deciding whether that made me a woodworker yet. (It didn't.)

> Plan: one bench, four legs, one seat, no surprises.
{: .callout .note}

## Day one: sawing

The first lesson arrived in the first ten minutes: wood does not care about your plan. Straight lines are an act of will.

{% include figure.html src="sawing.jpg" alt="Wood grain on a workbench during sawing." caption="Day one. The line looked straight when I drew it." align="wide" %}

> Day 6: four legs cut. Three of them are the same length.
{: .callout .update}

## The wobbly joint

This is the chapter I'd skip if I were being polite.

{% include figure-pair.html a="wobbly-joint.jpg" a_alt="Close view of wood grain at a joint." a_caption="Attempt one." b="sawing.jpg" b_alt="Wood grain on the bench top." b_caption="Attempt two (the repair)." %}

> The first joint was loose enough to rattle. I glued it, clamped it, and prayed.
{: .callout .fail}

> Clamps are a personality trait. Once you own six of them you can't stop.
{: .callout .find}

## The finished bench

After about three months of weekends, it exists.

{% include figure.html src="finished-bench.jpg" alt="The finished wooden bench seen from the side." caption="It holds weight. It has opinions about balance." align="center" %}

> Wobble is not failure. It's the bench telling you what to fix.
{: .callout .lesson}
