We applaud spontaneity quite a lot. We seem to idolize the people that move through life with no care in the world. The kind of people that are up for anything - If you call them up at 2:00 a.m. and ask if they wanna roam around the city, you can be sure their answer will always be - Hell yes. The kind of people that decide where they wanna go at the station. The ones that leave home in a whim to go on a month long solo trip. The ones that put no more than 2 minutes of thought into deciding to color their hair blue. They seem to light up rooms, become the center of every party they attend. And rightfully so. They have the best stories - of failure, of regret, of adrenaline rush, of euphoria.

After all, isn't that the point of life? To live life to the fullest, to live every day as if it was the last?

You'll find amazing philosophic angles in the minds of these people. They don't want to regret not having taken any opportunities that were sent their way. They chase the novelty, the excitement, the sense of feeling alive. The idea of not having any stories to tell their grandchildren terrifies them.

I do think novelty is the very essence of being alive. Our lives are what we make of them and the stories we tell ourselves in the meanwhile. A simple filter I like to use is - If there was a movie made on your life, would it be interesting enough to watch? For most of us - the answer will be a flat no.

But some of it is in our hands too. We could choose to push ourselves out of our tiny nooks of comfort and explore the world of opportunities out there. The internet likes to call it - Using your free will. Although this term has been used for a plethora of wrong reasons, the core idea is quite inspiring. You only have one life. Show up for the challenge you’ve been scared to take up, make a fool of yourself in the process, walk home in a different route today, strike up a conversation with a stranger, go on that solo trip. We all should introduce curiosity and novelty to our lives ****to the best of our abilities.

This is the part that always trips me up. The best of our abilities. This is where circumstances and lives differ. For a lot of people, there is a very clear answer to the question - "What's stopping you from living your best life?"

For most girls in our country, the answer is - Society. They are caged in fear of the boys that roam free. Can you blame a girl for not having the spontaneity to go on an impromptu solo trip when the world turns a blind eye whenever her safety is violated? Can you applaud a boy for having the spontaneity to go on a run at 10 a.m. without acknowledging the invisible permissions he was granted long before he made the choice?

For the people stuck in poverty, the answer is - Financial deficit. Deciding to switch careers at the age of 35 requires courage, sure. But more than that, it requires funds to sustain yourself while you learn a new trade.

For new parents, the answer is - their children. I’m sure it feels nice to go on a long drive at 2 am and enjoy street food at every stoppage. But can you really blame two constantly exhausted sleep-deprived parents for not being spontaneous enough to go on a long drive in the middle of the night, while constantly having to check the baby monitor in paranoia?

Spontaneity surely requires a level of curiosity and explorative nature. It requires constantly challenging yourself and pushing yourself out of your comfort zone. But I think it’s also very important to acknowledge what an incredible privilege it is to have the flexibility to think outside the box. I think this acknowledgement comes with empathy for the lesser fortunate and gratefulness for the privileges you do have. We are all here on earth to explore. All of us are on a lifelong quest to discover ourselves. Life was never about competing on how many wild stories we can compile. Comparing yourself to people that had obvious advantages will never bring joy to you. You may lack certain privileges, but life is the most beautiful when you look at it through the lens of wonder. Regardless of circumstances, we all have that lens within our hearts, why not put the glasses on?

May we never get so wrapped up in comparison that we forget the whole point. Perhaps the measure of a life was never how far we could wander, but how much wonder we could find within the distance we were given
